# OTA Watchdog — 2-Minute Demo Script

**Setup:** App running at `http://localhost:8001`. Bedrock is optional; if `BEDROCK_MODEL_ID` is configured and the generated output passes validation, Claude provides the operational brief. Otherwise the deterministic fallback is used.
May → June cycle completes automatically at startup.

---

## Opening (15 s)

**Say:** "OTA Watchdog is a deterministic agentic intelligence layer for transport
operations. The moment you start it, it runs a SENSE–REASON–ACT cycle and tells
you which vendors have materially deteriorated in on-time arrival — with every
number grounded in the trip data."

**Click:** Nothing yet.

---

## Step 1 — Show the proactive result (20 s)

**Say:** "The startup cycle has already completed. You can see the three phases —
SENSE, REASON, ACT — all green."

**Point to:** The phase pills (SENSE ✓ → REASON ✓ → ACT ✓).

**Say:** "The system ingested three months of anonymised ride data, applied volume
eligibility, computed OTA, and compared May to June. No manual assembly required."

**Point to the metrics bar:**

> 21 eligible vendors · 18 deteriorations · Fleet OTA 46.98% → 41.14% (−5.84 pts)

---

## Step 2 — Open a vendor alert (25 s)

**Say:** "Let me open one of the 18 alerts."

**Click:** Meera Pavlov Travel card.

**Say:** "Every number here is deterministic — computed from the trip records, not
generated by a model."

**Point to Vendor Evidence:**

> OTA 76.39% → 68.53% · change −7.86 pts · 5,294 current trips

**Point to Fleet Context:**

> Fleet moved −5.84 pts · 18 of 21 vendors crossed the threshold

---

## Step 3 — Recorded delay context (20 s)

**Say:** "The system also surfaces the recorded delay distribution — but we are
careful here."

**Point to Recorded Delay Context:**

> 1,666 late trips · NODELAY 1,518 (91.1%) · TRAFFIC 97 (5.8%)

**Say:** "91.1% of late trips were labeled NODELAY. The supplied data does not
define NODELAY as a missing reason, so the system treats it only as
recorded context. We show it without claiming it caused the OTA change.
The system uses 'Recorded delay context shows' — never 'TRAFFIC
caused deterioration.'"

---

## Step 4 — Operational brief (20 s)

**Say:** "This operational brief is generated from the structured evidence only. When Bedrock is configured and the output passes validation, Claude produces the wording. If Bedrock is unavailable or the output fails validation, the application falls back deterministically."

**Point to:** The Operational Brief section.

**Say:** "The narrative layer receives only the OTA numbers, fleet context, and delay counts you just saw. It cannot alter those values, cannot determine whether a threshold crossing occurred, and cannot infer causality. Its role is purely to convert verified evidence into a paragraph a Transport Manager can act on."

---

## Step 5 — June → July zero-alert state (20 s)

**Close** the modal.

**Click** the June → July tab.

**Click** Run Agent Cycle.

**Say:** "June to July — zero alerts."

**Point to:** The empty feed with the green checkmark.

**Say:** "All 21 eligible vendors maintained OTA within the threshold. This is a
legitimate outcome. The system does not manufacture alerts when the data doesn't
support them. The threshold-crossing decision is deterministic — the model has no influence
over whether this list is empty or not."

---

## Closing (10 s)

**Say:** "Five milestones, 254 automated tests, guarded Bedrock integration, and deterministic fallback. The deterministic layer is the foundation — Claude can make verified evidence more actionable without controlling the decision."

---

## Contingency — Bedrock unavailable

If Bedrock is not configured or unreachable, or if generated wording fails semantic or numeric validation, the Operational Brief section shows the deterministic fallback narrative instead. All OTA evidence, fleet context, and delay data remain fully functional.

**Say:** "The deterministic agent and evidence continue to function; the application
falls back to its deterministic brief."
